    :bdg-primary-line:`Abstract`

    .. container:: sd-badge-next-text

        In this talk, I will illustrate the critical role of Mathematical Models
        in the understanding, design and optimization of coronary stents.
        
        Stents are devices intended to reopen a coronalry occlusion that may
        lead to infarct. After the first implantation in humans at the end of
        the Seventies, design of stents has improved significantly mainly thanks
        to in vitro and in vivo studies. At the current level of sophistication,
        more accurate and rigorous mathematical models are needed for the next
        significant improvements and for avoiding market failures like
        bioresorbable stents. Established and more recent approaches, like the
        ones introduced by model reduction, can provide the breakthrough to
        improve coronary disease therapy, together with accurate data
        assimilation procedures to enable in silico clinical trials. In this
        talk, I will report my experience at Emory, GA Tech and North East
        Georgia Healthcare during the last 10 years.

        Jimena Martin-Tempestti (Emory), Brooks Lindsey (GA Tech).
